### Information Status

- Known:
  - The project is a professional services website for `Gabriel Demargne`.
  - The site needs to help people understand what is offered, trust the ability behind it, and contact Gabriel by email.
  - Primary audiences are:
    - low-tech clients who need a new website or improvements to an existing one
    - people seeking photography services
    - founders or small businesses needing design, UX/UI, and practical digital help
  - The site should be minimal, factual, and non-promotional.
  - Copy should avoid inflated claims, sales language, and weak trust signals like announcing a small project count.
  - The first version should be a multi-page site.
  - Photography is a real service, but the current photography site is also a personal gallery/archive.
  - The current UX portfolio lives inside the photography-led site, which is structurally wrong for a services-first business site.
  - Evidence already exists in finished websites and live work, including:
    - `maggie-hughes-art.com`
    - `richmondboatservices.com`
    - `impactinvestmentconsultants.com`
    - `mdemargne.com`
    - `buse.it`
    - `gabrieldemargne.com` for photography/gallery material
  - People should contact Gabriel via email.
  - Core offer areas currently identified are:
    - websites
    - UX / UI consulting
    - photography
    - small-business consulting around positioning and strategy

- Unknown:
  - Whether testimonials exist and are usable.
  - Whether past clients should be named broadly across the site.
  - Whether specific sectors or geographies should be emphasized for SEO.
  - Whether there is a preferred personal positioning line beyond the service categories.
  - Whether the UX portfolio should be a standalone page or a subsection within Selected Work.

- Assumptions:
  - Most leads will arrive with limited technical confidence and need clarity more than persuasion.
  - The main hesitation is not "is this person talented?" but "can Gabriel take a vague need and turn it into a finished, usable result?"
  - Photography should remain visible as a service, but the broader gallery/archive should not be the main business entry point.
  - UX/UI proof should be integrated into the main service site and no longer buried inside photography.
  - A restrained multi-page structure will create more confidence than a single long landing page.

- Risks created by assumptions:
  - If leads are actually hiring mainly for one discipline, the site could feel too broad.
  - If photography is given too much space, it may obscure the web/design offer.
  - If photography is minimized too much, it may underrepresent a meaningful service and distinctive part of the practice.
  - If the site is too sparse, it may feel unresolved rather than experienced.

- Conservative design response:
  - Lead with the clearest service offer and selected proof.
  - Separate business-hiring evidence from browsing-oriented gallery material.
  - Keep photography present but structurally secondary to the main services proposition.
  - Use factual copy, restrained hierarchy, and real examples instead of claims.
  - Prefer a small number of pages with clear jobs over one blended portfolio/archive.

- Clarifications requested:
  - None required to draft v1.
  - Testimonial availability and SEO/location details can be resolved later.

- Decisions made despite missing information:
  - Recommend one main professional services site.
  - Recommend keeping the existing photography archive/gallery as a separate destination linked from the new site.
  - Recommend absorbing UX portfolio material into the services site.

### 1. Strategy

- Website purpose:
  - Present Gabriel Demargne's professional services clearly and credibly so suitable clients can understand the offer and contact him.

- Primary users:
  - Non-technical or low-technical-confidence clients needing a website.
  - Small businesses or founders needing digital design, UX/UI, or practical strategic help.
  - People considering Gabriel for photography work.

- User needs:
  - Clear understanding of what is offered.
  - Enough proof to trust capability.
  - A simple path to contact.
  - Separation between professional evidence and personal/archive browsing.

- Site owner objectives:
  - Reposition from a gallery/portfolio-led impression to a services-led one.
  - Keep the tone minimal and factual.
  - Preserve photography as part of the offer without letting it dominate.
  - Surface UX/UI work in the right context.

- Functional problem:
  - Current material exists, but it is distributed across a photography-led site and separate live projects, making the professional offer harder to read quickly.

- Constraints:
  - Minimal, non-promotional tone.
  - No inflated claims or weak self-quantification.
  - Existing photography archive should remain meaningful.
  - Multi-disciplinary offer must still feel coherent.

- Success criteria:
  - A first-time visitor can quickly understand the offer.
  - The site feels credible without aggressive copy.
  - Relevant examples are easy to find.
  - Photography and UX/UI sit in the right roles.
  - Email contact is obvious and frictionless.

- Non-goals:
  - Acting like an agency site.
  - Maximizing marketing copy or conversion gimmicks.
  - Combining every body of work into one undifferentiated portfolio.
  - Building a giant content-heavy SEO machine in v1.

### 2. Scope

- Required functions:
  - Present services.
  - Present selected work/examples.
  - Present UX/UI proof.
  - Present photography as a service.
  - Link to the fuller photography archive/gallery.
  - Provide direct email contact.

- Required content:
  - Plain-language service descriptions.
  - Selected examples of completed websites and relevant projects.
  - UX/UI portfolio material.
  - Selected photography examples.
  - Short personal/professional framing.
  - Contact details.

- User questions the site must answer:
  - What does Gabriel actually do?
  - Is he relevant for my kind of project?
  - Can he handle both thinking and execution?
  - Is photography part of the professional offer or a separate passion?
  - How do I contact him?

- Required actions:
  - Navigate between services and proof.
  - Open examples.
  - Email Gabriel.

- Required states:
  - Standard page states only for v1.
  - If external links are used for legacy/archive work, they should be clearly labeled.
  - Empty/loading/error states matter only if dynamic content is introduced later.

- MVP boundary:
  - Small multi-page brochure-style site with evidence and contact.
  - Static content is acceptable for v1.
  - No CMS is required at this stage unless later maintenance needs justify it.

- Deferred opportunities:
  - Testimonials.
  - Detailed case studies.
  - Location/service-area pages for SEO.
  - A more formal process page.
  - A CMS or structured editing workflow.

- Things the site should not do:
  - Feel like a photo blog.
  - Read like startup copywriting.
  - Over-explain obvious things.
  - Present decorative filler as substance.

### 3. Structure

- Key user journeys:
  - A website lead lands, understands the offer, checks examples, then emails.
  - A UX/UI lead lands, finds focused design/product proof, then emails.
  - A photography lead lands, understands it is a service, reviews selected work, then either emails or browses the fuller archive.
  - A curious visitor uses selected work to understand range across disciplines.

- Task-specific stages:
  - Orient
  - Identify relevant service
  - Verify with examples
  - Contact

- Decision points:
  - "Is Gabriel relevant to my project?"
  - "Which of his services fits what I need?"
  - "Do the examples show the kind of thinking or execution I trust?"
  - "Do I continue into deeper work or contact now?"

- Information architecture:
  - `Home`
  - `Services`
  - `Selected Work`
  - `UX / UI`
  - `Photography`
  - `Contact`

- Page or section roles:
  - `Home`: orient, state the offer plainly, show selected proof, route to deeper areas.
  - `Services`: explain the offer categories without hype.
  - `Selected Work`: cross-disciplinary examples tied to hiring relevance.
  - `UX / UI`: focused evidence for product/design credibility.
  - `Photography`: service framing plus selected examples, with a link to the fuller archive/gallery.
  - `Contact`: simple email route and minimal context.

- Navigation model:
  - Quiet persistent top navigation.
  - No heavy portfolio taxonomy on first pass.
  - Primary nav should privilege services and proof over archive browsing.

- Content hierarchy:
  - Service clarity first.
  - Evidence second.
  - Deeper archive/accessory material after that.

- Persistent elements:
  - Clear navigation.
  - Visible contact route.
  - Consistent service/proof relationship.

- Hidden or delayed elements:
  - Full photography archive stays one step deeper.
  - Longer background/process content can be secondary.

### 4. Skeleton

- Layout approach:
  - Calm, editorial, service-led page structures with distinct jobs per page.
  - First screens should orient and route, not market theatrically.
  - Use examples as evidence close to the relevant service claims.

- First-screen responsibilities:
  - State what Gabriel offers in plain language.
  - Establish range without confusion.
  - Show at least one immediate path into evidence.
  - Keep email contact easy to find.

- Main interaction patterns:
  - Read, scan, compare, then email.
  - Light navigation between service pages and proof pages.
  - External/archive transitions clearly signposted.

- Component inventory, derived from requirements:
  - quiet site header/navigation
  - concise service lists
  - selected project/example blocks
  - image/media blocks
  - factual metadata rows
  - simple contact callouts
  - restrained footer

- What needs comparison:
  - Different services.
  - Different example types.
  - Possibly different roles within projects.

- What needs proof:
  - Website and design execution.
  - UX/UI thinking.
  - Photography quality and relevance.
  - Breadth without chaos.

- What needs quiet reading:
  - Service descriptions.
  - Personal framing/about copy.
  - Any factual location/SEO information.

- What can be reduced:
  - Marketing framing.
  - Repeated intros.
  - Decorative card systems.
  - Redundant sectioning.

- Usability risks:
  - Too much category overlap.
  - Visitors misreading photography as the primary business.
  - Sparse content feeling under-explained.
  - Too many examples without enough interpretation.

### 5. Surface

- Minimum visual treatment required:
  - Minimal, composed, confident, and clearly structured.
  - Enough hierarchy and contrast to feel deliberate, not bare.
  - Surface should support credibility, not mood-board styling.

- Typography behavior:
  - Clear hierarchy with restrained variation.
  - Comfortable body reading.
  - Headings that feel assured rather than promotional.

- Color behavior:
  - Mostly neutral base.
  - One restrained accent only if it helps orientation or emphasis.
  - Avoid anything glossy, startup-like, or atmospheric for its own sake.

- Imagery or evidence strategy:
  - Use real work as evidence.
  - Service pages should show relevant examples, not decorative filler.
  - Photography page should use selected examples with a route to the fuller archive.
  - UX/UI proof should be framed by what it demonstrates, not dumped as gallery fragments.

- Motion and interaction feedback:
  - Subtle only.
  - Enough for orientation and hover/active states.
  - No scroll theatrics or attention-seeking transitions.

- What should stay plain:
  - Contact.
  - Factual service copy.
  - SEO/location information.
  - Navigation.

- What would be over-design:
  - Centered landing-page hero tropes.
  - Big sales statements.
  - Card grids everywhere.
  - Heavy decoration to compensate for sparse copy.

### Artifact Safety Check

- Old artifacts that could leak into this project:
  - The current gallery-led homepage logic.
  - The portfolio/archive taxonomy from `Places`, `Projects`, and `UX Portfolio`.
  - Photography-first pacing.
  - Previous navigation mechanics designed for browsing rather than hiring.

- Decisions intentionally avoided:
  - Reusing the current photography site structure as the new services homepage.
  - Letting the UX portfolio remain buried inside photography.
  - Leading with a generic marketing hero.
  - Treating the gallery as the main proof model for every service.

- Why this structure belongs to the current function:
  - The main job is to help potential clients understand the offer, trust it, and contact Gabriel.
  - That requires service-first orientation, then carefully selected evidence, then deeper browsing.
  - The chosen structure separates hiring proof from archive browsing without discarding either.

- What makes the design unique to this brief:
  - It has to reconcile a real services business, a photography practice, and an existing UX portfolio without sounding like an agency or a portfolio dump.
  - The factual tone and the decision not to self-quantify early work materially shape the structure.
  - The gallery is treated as supporting evidence and a parallel archive, not the central identity.
